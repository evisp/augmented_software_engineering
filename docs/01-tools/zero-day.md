Before you write any code, you need your accounts working and you need
to know how learning works here. This page covers both. Do it before
your first day, not during it.

## Why this matters

Holberton works differently from a classroom. Nobody lectures you
through the material, and nobody hands you the answer. If you start
expecting that, the first weeks feel confusing. If you know the method,
they feel fast.

The accounts matter for a plainer reason: they are how your work gets
seen and checked. A broken login in week one turns into a missing
submission in week two.

## How learning works at Holberton

You learn by building. Every concept arrives as a project with a
deadline, and you work out how to solve it with your peers, the
documentation and your AI tools. The project is the lesson.

**Build.** You write the solution yourself. Searching, reading docs and
asking for help are all part of the work; copying a finished answer is
not.

**Check.** The Checker runs your code against the project's tests and
tells you straight away what passes and what fails. Fix, push, check
again.

**Review.** Your peers read your code and ask why it works the way it
does. You do the same for them, and you learn as much from reading
their solutions as from writing yours.

**Explain.** You should be able to justify every line you submit. That
is the skill employers test in interviews, and it is the skill AI makes
more valuable, not less.

### Your week

<div class="grid cards cols-3" markdown>

-   :material-calendar-check-outline: **Work every day**

    ---

    A little every day beats a long weekend session. Projects have
    deadlines and build on each other, so falling behind compounds.

-   :material-account-group-outline: **Peer learning days**

    ---

    You work through problems with your cohort, review each other's
    code and explain your solutions out loud.

-   :material-account-tie-outline: **Mentor days**

    ---

    Time with mentors to unblock hard problems, get feedback on your
    approach and prepare for interviews.

!!! ai "AI in the loop"

    AI is a collaborator here, not a shortcut. It drafts quickly, but it
    cannot check its own assumptions or take responsibility for the
    result. You can, and that is the job. On the intranet you also have
    **Clippy**, Holberton's own assistant. It helps you reason through a
    task step by step instead of giving you the code. You set up all of
    your assistants in the [AI Assistant](ai-assistant.md) tutorial.

The intranet and Slack share one login. GitHub is your own account, and
it is the one that follows you after you finish the program.

## Step 1: Set up the intranet

Go to [intranet.hbtn.io](https://intranet.hbtn.io/) and log in with the
credentials from your welcome email. Bookmark the page. You will open it
every day.

Then finish your profile:

1. Click your profile icon in the bottom left corner.
2. Fill in every field marked with an asterisk.
3. Add a photo. A plain head and shoulders shot is fine. This is the
   photo your mentors and future employers will see.
4. Save.

!!! warning "Do not change your password yet"

    Slack reads your account from the intranet. Change the password now
    and you may lock yourself out of Slack before it has synced. Wait
    until everything on this page works.

## Step 2: Join Slack

Open the intranet and find the Slack link in the left navigation panel.
Log in with the same credentials.

You are added to your cohort channel automatically. Install the desktop
or phone app as well. The browser tab is easy to forget, and Slack is
where schedule changes get announced.

## Step 3: Create your GitHub account

Already have one you use professionally? Use it and skip to Step 4.

Otherwise go to [github.com/signup](https://github.com/signup). Two
choices matter here.

**The email.** Use one you will still read in five years. Not a work or
school address that gets shut off when you leave.

**The username.** It becomes part of your professional identity and
appears in every project link you send to an employer. Something close
to your real name works well. Something you thought was funny at
nineteen does not.

When you have it, go back to your intranet profile and add the
username there. The Checker uses it to find your repositories, so a
typo means your projects do not get checked.

## Step 4: Get your laptop ready

You write and run all your code on your own laptop, inside Ubuntu Linux.
On Windows that means WSL, which is required for this program. You set
it up in the next tutorial. Before then, check two things:

- You can install software on the laptop. You need administrator rights.
- Windows is up to date: Windows 11, or Windows 10 version 2004 or later.

!!! note "On a Mac or Linux?"

    You already have a Unix terminal. Skip the WSL tutorial and go
    straight to [VS Code](vscode-setup.md).

## Check yourself

Five minutes, and it proves everything works.

1. Log in to the intranet and confirm your profile shows your photo and
   your GitHub username.
2. Post a short hello in your cohort channel on Slack.
3. Open your GitHub profile page and check the username reads the way
   you want an employer to read it.
4. Open any project on the intranet and find Clippy.

If any of these fails, ask in Slack now. That is exactly what the
channel is for.
