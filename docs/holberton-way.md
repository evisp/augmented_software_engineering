# The Holberton Way

<hr class="h-rule">

No lectures. Real projects. You own your learning.
{ .hero__kicker }

Nobody will stand at a board and teach you here. You build, you get
stuck, you get yourself unstuck, and that is the skill. Your peers help
you think, and mentors help when you have really tried. This page is how
it works, and what it asks of you.

[When you're stuck](#when-youre-stuck){ .h-button }
[See the roadmap](roadmap.md){ .h-button .h-button--ghost }

</div>

## Four ideas to remember

<div class="grid cards cols-4" markdown>

-   :material-account-arrow-up-outline: **You own your learning**

    ---

    The projects, this handbook and the people around you are there to
    help. The learning itself is your work, every day.

-   :material-weight-lifter: **Struggle is part of the work**

    ---

    Being stuck is not a sign that something is wrong. Getting unstuck
    on your own is exactly what employers pay for.

-   :material-help-circle-outline: **Ask well, at the right time**

    ---

    Not after two minutes, not after two days. Try for real first, then
    ask a clear question.

-   :material-comment-check-outline: **Explain what you ship**

    ---

    If you cannot explain a line, it does not go in. Not from a peer, not
    from the internet, not from an AI.

</div>

## Who does what

Learning here is a team effort, and everyone on the team has a
different job.

<div class="grid cards cols-3" markdown>

-   :material-account-outline: **You**

    ---

    - Work on your projects every day
    - Read the task, the docs and the tutorials
    - Build, test, debug and commit
    - Try before you ask, then ask clearly
    - Explain your code to others

-   :material-account-group-outline: **Your peers**

    ---

    - Think through problems with you
    - Pair program and review your code
    - Ask you "why?" until it is clear
    - Learn from your solutions, as you learn from theirs

-   :material-account-tie-outline: **Your mentors**

    ---

    - Unblock you when you are truly stuck
    - Challenge your approach and your design
    - Give feedback on your work
    - Prepare you for interviews and the job

</div>

Mentors are there for what is hard, not for what is new. A mentor
session is most useful when you arrive with a specific problem you have
already worked on:

<div class="grid cols-2" markdown>

!!! success "Mentor sessions are for"

    - A blocker you have tried to solve, and can describe
    - A design question: *is there a better way to do this?*
    - Feedback on code that already works
    - Questions about careers, interviews and how the industry works

!!! failure "Mentor sessions are not for"

    - A topic you have not read about yet
    - A project you have not started
    - Writing your code, or checking every line of it
    - Questions that the task, the docs or a peer can answer in minutes

</div>

- **Read** the task and its learning objectives before you write a line.
  They tell you what you will be asked to explain.
- **Learn** the concepts here, and do the "Try it" exercises and the
  practice sets. They use different examples from your projects on
  purpose: the project is yours to solve.
- **Build** your solution, and commit small steps as you go.
- **Check** it with the Checker, fix, and check again.
- **Review** it with peers, and review theirs.
- **Explain** it: every line, every choice. That is what a review, a
  defense and a job interview will ask of you.

## When you're stuck

You will be stuck often. That is normal, and it is where most of the
learning happens. What matters is how you get out. Climb the ladder one
step at a time, and use your AI assistant alongside every step.

1. **Re-read.** The task and the error message, slowly, word by word.
   Half of all problems are solved right here: a wrong file name, a
   missed requirement, an error that says exactly what is wrong.
2. **Look it up.** The official docs, the `man` page, the tutorial in
   this handbook. Search for the exact error message.
3. **Debug.** Print the values you think you know. Shrink the problem to
   the smallest piece of code that still fails. Explain it out loud, to
   a peer or to a rubber duck: saying it often shows you the mistake.
4. **Ask your peers.** In the room or on Slack, with what you tried.
   Someone has often just solved the same problem, and explaining it
   helps them too.
5. **Mentor session.** When you and your peers are still stuck, bring a
   prepared question to a mentor.

**Your AI assistant works alongside every step**, not only as a last
resort: ask it what an error means, what to check next, or to quiz you
on a concept. Your general assistant and **Clippy**, Holberton's
assistant on the intranet, are both there for that. Clippy is built to
guide you through the reasoning rather than hand you code; use your
general assistant the same way.

!!! tip "How long before you ask?"

    Being stuck for twenty minutes is learning. Being stuck for two days
    is a waste. As a guide: give a problem around twenty minutes of
    *real* trying, with steps 1 to 3 and your assistant, before you go to
    your peers. If you are going in circles, ask sooner.

## Ask a good question

A good question gets a good answer fast, and writing it often solves
the problem on its own. Use this template, on Slack or in a session:

```text title="A good question"
What I'm trying to do:  ...
What I expected:        ...
What happened:          ... (the exact error, copied, not retyped)
What I've tried:        ...
The code:               ... (the smallest piece that shows the problem)
```

<div class="grid cols-2" markdown>

!!! failure "Hard to help"

    > My SQL doesn't work, can someone help?

    No task, no error, no code. Whoever answers has to ask five
    questions before they can start.

!!! success "Easy to help"

    > I'm counting loans per member with a `LEFT JOIN`. I expected Lea to
    > have 0, but she has 1. I tried `COUNT(*)` and `COUNT(member_id)`.
    > Here is the query: ...

    Anyone can see the problem in a minute, and you have probably
    spotted it yourself while writing.

</div>

**Before a mentor session**, check that you can tick all of these:

- [ ] I have climbed steps 1 to 4 of the ladder
- [ ] I can say what I am trying to do, in one sentence
- [ ] I have the exact error, and the smallest code that shows it
- [ ] I can say what I have already tried, and what happened
- [ ] My latest code is pushed, so we can look at it together


## AI, the Holberton way

AI is your amplifier, not your replacement. You will use it every day,
and these rules keep it working for you instead of against you:

<div class="grid cards cols-2" markdown>

-   :material-lightbulb-on-outline: **Reason with it**

    ---

    Ask it to explain errors, concepts and options. Ask it to quiz you.
    Use it to understand, not to skip understanding.

-   :material-account-check-outline: **You are responsible**

    ---

    It drafts; you decide. Whatever you commit is yours, including its
    mistakes.

-   :material-crystal-ball: **Predict before you run**

    ---

    Before you run code from an assistant, say what it should do. If the
    result surprises you, find out why.

-   :material-comment-question-outline: **Explain every line**

    ---

    If you cannot explain a line it gave you, ask it to explain, then
    rewrite it in your own words, or leave it out.

</div>

## Habits that work, habits that fail

<div class="grid cols-2" markdown>

!!! success "Habits that work"

    - Working a little every day
    - Reading the whole task before starting
    - Committing small steps, with clear messages
    - Asking after real trying, with a prepared question
    - Reading your peers' code, not only your own
    - Explaining your solution out loud before you submit it

!!! failure "Habits that fail"

    - Leaving everything for the weekend before the deadline
    - Starting to code before reading the task
    - One huge push the night before
    - Asking after two minutes, or suffering alone for two days
    - Copying a solution you cannot explain
    - Letting an AI write what you have not understood

</div>
